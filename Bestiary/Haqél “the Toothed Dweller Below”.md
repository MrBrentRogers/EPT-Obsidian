Deep lakes and streams, coastal flats.

This scaly, dull silver-hued creature drifts justbelowthe surface of a lake or tidal pool with its horn-rimmed mawopen waiting for an unwary victim to pass above (either swimming or in a small boat). It then leaps up, seizes its prey, and drags it off under the water. The Hagel fights with serried rows of razor-sharp teeth (made of ahorny substance) and also uses its mighty, finned tail to strike . The smaller varieties grow to about 15 feet in length; larger ones range up to 30 feet long ; and the largest known individuals are some 50 feet from snout to tail . The Hagel does not often attack larger vessels, but such occurrences are indeed known.
