A stampede of random beasts rushes towards and past the party. Anyone with a passive perception of 8+ will notice the stampede of beasts. Creatures who are aware of the stampede can move out of its way.

1. 3d4 boars (grassland/woodland/forest/hills)
2. 2d6 + 4 antelope/deer (grassland/woodland/forest/hills)
3. 2D4 horses (grassland/woodland/forest/hills)
4. 1d4 elephants/mammoths
5. 2d6+3 elk/moose with 1d6 giant/elk/moose
6. 2d6 + 2 boars with 1d4 giant boars
7. 4d4 mountain goats/rams
8. 1d8 + 3 rhinos/buffalo
9. 3d6 wolves/jackals
10. Roll twice and double size of encounter.

The stampede could have started from:

1. Running from a predator
2. Something scared them
3. Something is attracting theme (something on the party?)
4. They were commanded to go somewhere
5. They were commanded to attack someone (the party?)

If the stampede is not targeting creatures, the entire herd/pack cause stampede damage to everything in their path based on their size. Those caught in the stampede must succeed a Strength saving throw or take half damage.

Medium beasts 1d8 x (number of bests) Bludgeoning damage  
Large beasts 1d10 x (number of bests) Bludgeoning damage  
Huge beasts 1d12 x (number of bests) Bludgeoning damage.

Encounter Distances  
Below are guidelines on when to start an encounter based on terrain, audible distances, and outdoor visibility.

Terrain  
Encounter Distance  
Arctic, desert, farmland, or grassland 6d6 x 10 feet  
Forest, swamp, or woodland 2d8 x 10 feet  
Hills or wastelands 2d10 x 10 feet  
Jungle 2d6 x 10 feet  
Mountains 4d10 x 10 feet

Audible Distance  
Trying to be quiet 2d6 x 5 feet  
Normal noise level 2d6 x 10 feet  
Very loud 2d6 x 50 feet

Visibility Outdoors  
Clear day, no obstructions 2 miles  
Rain 1 mile  
Fog 100 to 300 feet  
From a height x 20
